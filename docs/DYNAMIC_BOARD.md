# Dynamic-Board Quoridor: Rules, Architecture, and Experiment Plan

This document specifies the rules, design, and pre-registered experimental evaluation for generalized Quoridor with dynamic board sizes ($7 \times 7$ to $12 \times 12$) and selectable player counts (2 or 4 players).

---

## 1. Rules

### 1.1 Board Dimensions and Coordinate System
- The board consists of an $N \times N$ grid of square cells, where $N \in \{7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12\}$.
- Cell coordinates are indexed as $(r, c)$ with row $r \in \{0, \dots, N-1\}$ and column $c \in \{0, \dots, N-1\}$. Row $0$ is the top side; column $0$ is the left side.
- Wall slots form an $(N-1) \times (N-1)$ grid of groove intersections indexed as $(r, c)$ with $r, c \in \{0, \dots, N-2\}$.
- A wall spans two cell edges and has an orientation $o \in \{\text{'H'}, \text{'V'}\}$.
  - A horizontal wall (`H`) at $(r, c)$ blocks vertical movement between $(r, c) \leftrightarrow (r+1, c)$ and $(r, c+1) \leftrightarrow (r+1, c+1)$.
  - A vertical wall (`V`) at $(r, c)$ blocks horizontal movement between $(r, c) \leftrightarrow (r, c+1)$ and $(r+1, c) \leftrightarrow (r+1, c+1)$.

### 1.2 Players, Seating, and Turn Order
- Games support either 2 or 4 players.
- In 4-player mode, players are indexed $0, 1, 2, 3$ seated clockwise:
  - Seat 0: Bottom side
  - Seat 1: Left side
  - Seat 2: Top side
  - Seat 3: Right side
  - Turn order proceeds clockwise: $0 \to 1 \to 2 \to 3 \to 0$.
- In 2-player mode, two players occupy seats 0 and 2 (Bottom and Top). Turn order proceeds $0 \to 2 \to 0$.

### 1.3 Start Cells and Quarter-Turn Symmetry
- Let $c_0 = \lfloor (N - 1) / 2 \rfloor$.
- Player 0 starts at cell $(N - 1, c_0)$.
- Successive seats are generated by rotating the starting cell clockwise by 90 degrees around the center of the board via the transformation $(r, c) \mapsto (c, N - 1 - r)$:
  - Seat 0 (Bottom): $(N - 1, c_0)$
  - Seat 1 (Left): $(c_0, 0)$
  - Seat 2 (Top): $(0, N - 1 - c_0)$
  - Seat 3 (Right): $(N - 1 - c_0, N - 1)$
- **Odd $N$:** Every pawn starts at the exact midpoint column or row of its side (for $N = 9$, $c_0 = 4$, yielding standard cells $(8, 4)$, $(4, 0)$, $(0, 4)$, and $(4, 8)$).
- **Even $N$:** The board lacks an exact center cell. Quarter-turn rotation preserves symmetry under 90-degree rotations in 4-player mode and 180-degree half-turn rotation in 2-player mode. In 2-player mode on even $N$, Seat 0 starts at $(N-1, c_0)$ and Seat 2 starts at $(0, N-1-c_0)$ in different columns.

### 1.4 Goal Sides and Victory Condition
- Each player aims to reach any cell on the opposite boundary edge:
  - Seat 0 (Bottom): Goal is row $0$ ($\{(0, c) \mid 0 \le c < N\}$).
  - Seat 1 (Left): Goal is column $N - 1$ ($\{(r, N - 1) \mid 0 \le r < N\}$).
  - Seat 2 (Top): Goal is row $N - 1$ ($\{(N - 1, c) \mid 0 \le c < N\}$).
  - Seat 3 (Right): Goal is column $0$ ($\{(r, 0) \mid 0 \le r < N\}$).
- The first player whose pawn steps onto any cell of their designated goal side wins immediately.
- There is no elimination and no resignation requirement.

### 1.5 Wall Inventory
- Default walls per player:
  - 2-player mode: $W_{2P}(N) = N + 1$
    - $N=7: 8$, $N=8: 9$, $N=9: 10$, $N=10: 11$, $N=11: 12$, $N=12: 13$.
  - 4-player mode: $W_{4P}(N) = \lceil (N + 1) / 2 \rceil$
    - $N=7: 4$, $N=8: 5$, $N=9: 5$, $N=10: 6$, $N=11: 6$, $N=12: 7$.
- The wall count is parameterized via `walls_per_player` for custom configurations and ablation experiments.

### 1.6 Pawn Movement and Jumping Rules
- A player may move their pawn one step orthogonally (up, down, left, right) to an adjacent cell if movement is not blocked by a wall or the board boundary.
- If an adjacent cell is occupied by another pawn:
  1. **Straight Jump:** The pawn may jump directly over the adjacent pawn into the cell immediately beyond it, provided the step to the adjacent pawn and the step to the landing cell are unblocked by walls, and the landing cell is on the board and unoccupied by any third pawn.
  2. **Diagonal Jump:** If the straight jump is blocked (by a wall, the board edge, or another pawn standing behind), the pawn may jump diagonally to either of the two cells beside the jumped pawn, provided that landing cell is on the board, unoccupied, and not separated by a wall from the jumped pawn.
  3. No multi-jump combinations or chains are permitted within a single turn.

### 1.7 Wall Legality and Fair-Play Invariant
- A wall move is legal if and only if:
  1. The player has at least one wall remaining in their inventory.
  2. $0 \le r \le N - 2$, $0 \le c \le N - 2$, and $o \in \{\text{'H'}, \text{'V'}\}$.
  3. For $o = \text{'H'}$: No overlap with $(r, c), (r, c-1), (r, c+1)$ in existing horizontal walls, and no cross with $(r, c)$ in vertical walls.
  4. For $o = \text{'V'}$: No overlap with $(r, c), (r-1, c), (r+1, c)$ in existing vertical walls, and no cross with $(r, c)$ in horizontal walls.
  5. **Fair-play invariant:** After placement, every active player must retain at least one unblocked path to their target goal side. Path connectivity is evaluated using BFS over cell graphs ignoring pawn presence.

### 1.8 Pass Rule
- In 4-player Quoridor, a player in a corner or cul-de-sac with 0 remaining walls whose adjacent cells and jump landings are blocked by opponent pawns and walls has 0 legal moves.
- When a player has zero legal pawn moves and zero legal wall placements, the only legal move is `PassMove`.
- The interface reports "No legal move, turn skipped."

### 1.9 Ranking and Turn Cap
- **Ranking:** The winner is rank 1. Remaining players are ranked in ascending order of their remaining shortest-path BFS distance to their goal side. Tied players share the average rank (e.g. ties for 2nd and 3rd share rank 2.5).
- **Turn Cap:** Default total plies is $40 \times N$ (configurable via `max_turns`). If the cap is reached without a winner, `no_winner=True` is recorded and all players are ranked strictly by shortest-path distance.
- **Game-Theoretic Nature:** Four-player Quoridor is general-sum (non-zero-sum). A victory for one player is a loss for three, introducing tactical kingmaking, leader-targeting, and coalition dynamics.

## 2. Design

### 2.1 Engine (`quoridor_n/`)
- `GameConfig`: Configuration dataclass holding `board_size`, `num_players`, `walls_per_player`, and `max_turns`.
- `GameState`: Immutable dataclass parameterized by `GameConfig`, tracking pawn positions, remaining wall counts, horizontal and vertical wall sets, current player, and turn count.
- `quoridor_n/pathfinding.py`:
  - `shortest_path(state, player)`: Single-player forward BFS returning cell path or None.
  - `shortest_path_length(state, player)`: Returns integer step count or None.
  - `has_path(state, player)`: Boolean path existence test.
  - `multi_source_distances(state, player)`: Multi-source BFS backward from player's goal side, yielding an $N \times N$ distance array.
- `quoridor_n/rules.py`: Move validation, pawn stepping, straight and diagonal jumps, wall collision checks, and pass handling.
- `quoridor_n/moves.py`: Candidate wall generation pruning with `wall_target` options (`all`, `leader`, `next`, `none`).
- `quoridor_n/runner.py`: `play_game(agents, config, ...)` returning detailed game outcome records.
- `quoridor_n/render.py`: ASCII board rendering for any $N \in [7, 12]$.

### 2.2 Agent Implementations (`quoridor_n/agents/`)
- `RandomAgent`: Selects random legal pawn steps with wall probability $p_{\text{wall}}$ (default 0.2).
- `GreedyAgent`: Steps along its shortest path to the goal; places walls with probability $p_{\text{wall}}$ (default 0.0) targeting `wall_target`.
- `ParanoidAgent`: Adversarial alpha-beta search. Maximizes root player's score on own turn; assumes all opponents form a coalition to minimize root player's score on their turns. In 2-player mode, reduces to standard minimax. Options: `depth`, `use_move_ordering`, `use_wall_pruning`, `use_tt`, `wall_cap`, `eval_mode`, `wall_target`, `seed`.
- `MaxnAgent`: Multi-player search returning an evaluation vector across active players. Each player maximizes their own component. Includes transposition tables and move ordering. (Available in 4-player mode only).
- `MCTSAgent`: UCT Monte Carlo Tree Search for 2 or 4 players.
  - Parameters: `iterations`, `c` (1.4), `rollout_depth` ($2N + 20$), `rollout_policy` (`path_biased` or `random`), `rollout_greedy_share` (0.95), `cutoff_scoring` (`vs_mean`, `vs_leader`), `use_wall_pruning`, `wall_target`, `seed`.
  - **Wall-Respecting BFS Rollouts:** Rollout policy uses shortest-path BFS distance that respects physical walls. With probability `rollout_greedy_share` (0.95), steps along the wall-respecting shortest path; otherwise selects uniformly among candidate moves (pawn moves and pruned candidate walls).

### 2.3 Server and Interface Architecture
- `server/app.py`: FastAPI server supporting dynamic `board_size`, `num_players`, multiple `opponents`, and difficulty tiers (`Easy`, `Medium`, `Hard`).
- `web/`: Dynamic interface rendering scalable SVG/CSS grid boards from $7 \times 7$ to $12 \times 12$, supporting 2 or 4 players, touch previews, arrow key navigation, and rank summaries.

## 3. Experiment Plan and Pre-Registration

### 3.1 Pre-Registered Hypotheses
- **H1 (Skill Ladder):** Each search agent family (`paranoid`, `maxn`, `mcts`) achieves a win rate significantly higher than baseline chance against greedy opponents ($> 25\%$ in 4-player mode at $9 \times 9$, $> 50\%$ in 2-player mode at $9 \times 9$).
- **H2 (Family Differences at 4-Player Table):** At a balanced 4-player round-robin table at $9 \times 9$, agent families (`greedy`, `paranoid`, `maxn`, `mcts`) differ significantly in win rates and mean ranks.
- **H3 (2-Player Search Paradigm Comparison):** In 2-player mode at $9 \times 9$, alpha-beta (`paranoid`) and MCTS matched for time per move exhibit a statistically significant difference in win rate.
- **H4 (Seat Bias / First-Mover Advantage):** Identical agents show a statistically significant first-mover advantage (Seat 0 win rate $> 25\%$ in 4-player, $> 50\%$ in 2-player) at $9 \times 9$.
- **H5 (Leader Targeting vs Placebo):** Search agents' wall placements lengthen the current game leader's shortest path significantly more often than a random candidate wall placebo baseline at the same positions.
- **H6 (Board Size Impact on Seat Bias):** First-mover advantage differs significantly between small boards ($7 \times 7$) and large boards ($12 \times 12$).
- **H7 (Computational Scaling):** Nodes evaluated and time per move scale super-linearly with board size $N \in [7, 12]$.
- **H8 (Ablation A1 - Wall Pruning Target):** Pruning walls to `leader` maintains comparable win rate to `all` while reducing search nodes per move.
- **H9 (Ablation A2 - Search Paradigm):** `paranoid` achieves higher win rate than `maxn` at matched depths due to deeper effective lookahead or conservative defense.
- **H10 (Ablation A3 - Search Enhancements):** Move ordering and transposition tables significantly reduce nodes expanded to reach fixed search depths.
- **H11 (Ablation A4 - Heuristic Evaluation Modes):** Evaluation modes (`mean`, `leader`, `own`) differ significantly in win rate against fixed opponents.
- **H12 (Ablation A5 - Wall Budget Sensitivity):** Smaller wall budgets (e.g. 3 walls) reduce game length and shift agent rankings compared to standard budgets.
- **H13 (Ablation A6 - MCTS Rollout Strategy):** Wall-respecting `path_biased` rollouts achieve significantly higher win rates than uniform `random` rollouts.

### 3.2 Statistical Methodology
- **Proportions:** 95% Wilson score confidence intervals.
- **Exact Binomial Tests:** Two-sided exact binomial tests against theoretical null baselines ($p_0 = 0.25$ for 4-player, $p_0 = 0.50$ for 2-player).
- **Pairwise Comparisons:** Paired sign tests for agents competing in the same games.
- **Multiple Comparisons:** Holm-Bonferroni correction applied across hypothesis families, reporting both raw and adjusted $p$-values.
- **Clustered Data:** Bootstrap resampling over games (not individual wall placements) for behavioral metrics (E4).
- **Power Sizing:** Minimum sample sizes chosen to achieve $> 80\%$ power at $\alpha = 0.05$ (e.g., $N \ge 168$ for 4-player round-robin permutations; $N \ge 100$ for 2-player tests).
